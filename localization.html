my role, in between

I came to localization from content: I'd been the technical writer whose words were being translated. That put me between engineering, product, finance, regional teams, and vendors, and let me fix problems at the source, in the English, before they multiplied by eight.

Strategize

What was the problem?

The company was expanding into Europe, starting with a high-stakes launch in France. There was no localization program: no vendor, no process for translating the interface, and few people who knew what internationalization required.

The goal: a program that keeps launching without heroics.

Strategize

Where did you start?

I studied how other companies did it, earned a software localization certificate, and set a strategy for France and every market after it.

Leadership handed me the France launch on trust. I went straight to detailed requirements, a vendor partner, and a backup plan for every step that could fail.

Build

How did you choose the platform?

With a seven-month selection process, built to compare tools on real work.

4 vendors
→
360 requirements
→
10 user personas
→
300+ test cases
→
1 winner

The winner passed more tests and cost less than half of the runner-up in year one. Then I moved every project onto it with shared terminology and translation memory, and consolidated duplicate contracts across the company.

Build

How did it scale without heroics?

Fix the source first

Translation can't fix broken English, so I set up a process for interface text and taught the company what internationalization needs.

Ship continuously

I rebuilt the build and deployment process with automated quality checks. Deployment got 86% faster, so translations kept pace with releases.

Built to run as a team

I kept accountability, vendors, and finances in-house and flexed everything else with partners. Then I hired a program manager, grew team leads, and started a company-wide localization community.

Sustain

Where did it go next?

After France, the program grew to 8 locales. One release added Spanish and Canadian French at once, our first multi-language launch. New products joined, and acquired companies reused the process.

By the end, new languages shipped quietly. That quiet was the whole point.

Where my AI work began

Machine translation with human review
→
Translation memory and shared terms
→
Language-quality rules (NLP)
→
LLMs scoring quality, routing risky content to linguists

Years before generative AI, localization taught me where machines help and where people stay essential. Those decisions stayed with the team.

Reflect

What would you do differently?

First launches are always imperfect, and I held ours to a standard that cost the team too much. Now I set "ship it, then hone it" expectations from day one, and protect the team's pace as carefully as the quality.

How I used AI

Localization is where my AI work started: machine translation with human review, NLP-powered quality rules, and later testing large language models to score translations and route only risky content to people. Choosing where human linguists were essential stayed a human decision.
