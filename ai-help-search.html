my role, in between

I was the product owner, and I also owned the content system and help experience the AI answered from. That put me at the center of content, conversation design, research, localization, data science, and engineering, all learning to launch an AI product together for the first time. Owning both the product and its source material gave me insight into why answers worked or failed that no one else on the team had.

Strategize

What was the problem?

Customers kept telling us finding help was one of their biggest barriers. Answers lived across separate sites, and keyword search made people guess the right words. The common wisdom said people hate chatbots. Our research found something more specific:

People hate not being heard.

So the goal: help that understands a question the way the customer asks it.

Strategize

How did you decide what to build?

With customers, before writing a line of code. We put a prototype in front of 50+ customers at our annual conference, and the 168 real questions they asked shaped everything after.

Build

How did you know the answers were good?

Engineering could test whether the system found the right content, but nobody was checking whether the final answer was right. So I defined "good" before launch: a human evaluation framework with seven weighted criteria, from accuracy to safety, and every miss traced to a root cause.

The same habit guided upgrades: before switching models, three of us scored four setups across four kinds of questions. The winner was 5–6 seconds slower per answer, and we chose that tradeoff on purpose.

Build

How did you launch without losing trust?

In stages. Early access with 31 companies came first. When the answer rate dipped, we studied every failed question and fixed the causes within a week. Then we rolled out to a fifth of customers at a time.

Sustain

Where did it go next?

The most-used option in that menu told us something important. It was “Help on this page,” which shows help for exactly where the customer is in the product.

Help that knows where you are beats help that makes you ask.

So once AI search was good enough, I set its finish line and shifted our focus back to context-sensitive help, the help infrastructure underneath it, and scale. To set that line, I analyzed 150,000 real questions from six months of use. Every item in the plan traces to something customers did.

Reflect

What would you do differently?

Quality turned out to be the real product. The work shifted from creating content to managing quality, so next time I'd staff for that from day one and define the end state before launch.

How I used AI

The product is AI (retrieval-augmented generation over help content). I co-drafted the quality framework with an AI research assistant and used AI to find patterns in 150,000 questions. The judgment calls stayed human: what "good" means, what to fix first, and where to stop.
