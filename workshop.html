my role, in between

I designed and facilitated the workshop, and I also led these ten people. So I held two roles in the room: the leader with a point of view, and the facilitator making space for everyone else's. Opening with their ideas before mine kept the vision theirs.

Strategize

What was the problem?

AI was changing how people use software, and our content experiences weren't ready. Help, in-product messages, learning, search, and community each lived in their own system, with their own data and their own team.

We had a lot of great two-person bands. We needed one band.

So instead of handing the group a strategy, I asked one question: how do we get to the future, all together?

Strategize

How did you prepare?

I defined success up front: a shared vision tied to business results, a handful of things we could move on our own, the dependencies to negotiate, and an honest look at how our jobs would change.

A month of pre-work meant everyone arrived with a point of view: 15+ webinars and trend scans, customer research, and a one-page vision from each leader, in their own voice.

Build

What made people own the plan?

Every design choice served one goal: people leave owning the plan.

Everyone arrives equal

Each leader's one-page vision opened day one, before mine, so no single discipline set the agenda.

Problems before technology

Focus on the customer problem and the business result, with no attachment to a tool.

Build it in the room

Teams prototyped as we went: help rewritten by AI for different roles, an AI feature dashboard, a content parser.

Redesign every night

Each evening I used AI to rebuild the board and the next day's plan from what actually happened.

Make it human

A shared soundtrack, a karaoke night, a gratitude wall. On day two, the group named trust as the missing layer in everything we built.

Sustain

Did anything happen after?

We ended with a proposal: every project had an owner, a realistic start date, and named support from product, engineering, research, and operations, plus 15+ things we'd pause or stop. Mini-squads, async demos, and a shared channel kept momentum.

7 of 12

projects in motion months later

1 talk

on the method, for the wider design organization

The workshop's ideas showed up in company strategy, and individual contributors started leading strategy work themselves.

Reflect

What would you do differently?

Four days creates momentum, and real change is slower. Next time I'd design the three months after the workshop as carefully as the workshop itself, with concrete next steps for the people who need them.

How I used AI

This was my first workshop at this scale, on a tight timeline, so I leaned on AI heavily: drafting the agenda, designing activities, and each night turning the day's board into a recap and a new plan. I set the goals, read the room, and ran the follow-up. AI holds structure well. The context and the trust came from the people in the room.
